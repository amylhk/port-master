# People and factions of Melaka, 1509–1511

## 1. How to use this research

- **[Documented fact]** means that the cited text attests the claim. “Brown says” or “Birch reports” documents an account, not necessarily the truth of its dialogue, accusation or motive.
- **[Scholarly inference]** means an editorial identification or a source-critical reconstruction; a named editor's judgment is distinguished from this document's own reading.
- **[Gameplay abstraction]** means suggested fiction or mechanics, not historical testimony. The citations on those suggestions identify the historical starting point, not evidence for the invented behavior.

**[Gameplay abstraction]** Use this roster for [Stage D: Narrative and Political Layer](../gameplay-blueprint.md#13-development-blueprint-from-vision-to-playable-game). Let its characters change what can move through the [Stage C economy](../gameplay-blueprint.md#13-development-blueprint-from-vision-to-playable-game) through favors, information and access, not through fixed ethnic loyalty scores. Use [1511-events.md §§1–2, 4](1511-events.md) for dates, the Portuguese cast (including Rui de Araújo) and contested events; [port-official.md §4](port-official.md#4-what-the-shahbandar-controlled-and-what-other-offices-controlled) for the offices and their jurisdictions.

**[Documented fact]** The evidence below spans 1509–1511, with later fates where the accounts supply them. Citations use printed English-edition pages, not scan image numbers. In Brown, the bracketed numbers are from the underlying Malay edition: for example, the purge is on Brown pp. 162–164, not pp. 186–188 (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 162–164).

**[Scholarly inference]** Keep a source field on disputed relationships. The *Annals* make the purge a story about favoritism, false testimony and loyalty; Pires relates it to the failed 1509 attack, the ruler's fear and the seizure of wealth; Albuquerque's *Commentaries* report Portuguese negotiations and present the conqueror's case against Utimutiraja. None is neutral access to the participants' private intentions (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 159–164; Pires II, pp. 257–259; Birch III, chs. XXXIII–XXXV, pp. 143–152).

## 2. Character dossiers

### Sultan Mahmud Shah (Pires's Mafamut; Birch's Mahamet)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Sultan of Melaka and Ahmad's father (Pires II, p. 254 and n. 3; Brown, ch. XXII, p. 166).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Malay royal house; in Pires's account he invokes defense of “our Holy Prophet” in council (Pires II, pp. 253–256).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] Pires depicts him accepting the Bendahara's and Gujarati lobby's counsel in 1509, then fearing the Bendahara's power; in 1511, Pires puts him at odds with advisers urging peace (Pires II, pp. 254–258, 280).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] In Pires he orders action against Sequeira; in both Pires and Brown he orders the purge. Brown has him withdraw and hand Ahmad “his officers and the regalia” before the conquest (Pires II, pp. 256–259; Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 163–166).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] Brown has him resume rule after Ahmad's death, hold court at Bentan and later die at Kampar (Brown, chs. XXIII, XXVIII, pp. 169–171, 191–194).
- **Source disagreements:** [Documented fact] Pires still calls Mahmud king and his son an adviser during the 1511 negotiations; Brown makes Ahmad the reigning and fighting sultan. Birch later says Mahamet had died at Pao and Alaoadim succeeded him, at odds with Brown's sequence (Pires II, p. 280; Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 166–170; Birch III, ch. XXXIII, pp. 143–144).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] The accounts do not establish a division of day-to-day port authority between the two rulers in 1511 (Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 166–169; Pires II, p. 280).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Keep Mahmud's patronage distinct from Ahmad's orders; let contacts disagree over whose authorization will hold (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 166; Pires II, p. 280).

### Raja / Sultan Ahmad Shah (Pires's Amet)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Mahmud's son and reigning sultan in Brown's account of the conquest (Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 166–169; Pires II, p. 254 n. 3).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Malay royal house (Brown, ch. XXIII, pp. 167–170).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] Brown names Tun Ali Hati, Tun Mai Ulat Bulu and Tun Muhammad Rahang among his favorites and sets them against the established chiefs in the narrative (Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 166, 169–170).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] Brown has Ahmad rally the defense at the bridge and suffer a wound; Pires includes Mahmud's son among the opponents of peace; Birch describes the “Prince of Malaca” establishing stockades at Muar (Brown, ch. XXIII, pp. 167–169; Pires II, p. 280; Birch III, ch. XXIX, pp. 128–131).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] Brown has him retreat to Bentan and be killed on his father's orders; Pires also says the father later killed the son (Brown, ch. XXIII, pp. 169–170; Pires II, p. 280).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] Do not turn Birch's “Alaoadim” in the Upeh correspondence story into an uncontested alternate name for Ahmad. Brown names a different son, Tun Fatimah's Alauddin, and places his designation after Ahmad's death (Birch III, ch. XXXIII, pp. 143–144; Brown, ch. XXIII, pp. 170–171).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] The young court circle is attested in Brown, but there is no roster of a formal war party or a port policy platform (Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 166–170; Pires II, p. 280).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Give Ahmad's household urgent defense requests that compete with commercial bookings rather than assigning it a permanent anti-trade ideology (Brown, ch. XXIII, pp. 167–169).

### Tun Fatimah

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Mutahir's daughter; first married her cousin Tun Ali, then became Mahmud's consort, styled “Raja Perempuan” in the *Annals* (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 159–160, 165–167; Pires II, pp. 258–259 and n. 2).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Melaka court, of the Bendahara household by birth and the royal household by remarriage (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 159–160, 165–167).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] Brown portrays her mourning her father and obtaining a promise from Mahmud that a son of hers would succeed him (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 166–167).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] Her first marriage precedes and her remarriage follows the purge in Brown; the text does not date either to a specific day or year (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 159–160, 163–166).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] Brown names their son Raja Alauddin Shah, designated Sultan Muda ahead of Raja Muzaffar; after Mahmud's death he succeeds as Sultan Alauddin Riayat Shah and establishes himself at Ujong Tanah (Johor). These passages give no death date for Tun Fatimah (Brown, chs. XXIII, XXVIII–XXIX, pp. 170–171, 193–195).
- **Source disagreements:** [Documented fact] Pires also reports Mahmud took the Bendahara's daughter as wife and had a son by her, but does not give Brown's account of her negotiations and the displacement of Muzaffar (Pires II, pp. 258–259; Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 166–167, 170–171).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] These accounts establish her dynastic position, not command of a merchant network; Brown's characterization is not a basis for a literal portrait (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 159–160, 165–167).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Use court correspondence about kin and succession; any warehouse contacts or delegations attributed to her are fictional (Brown, ch. XXIII, pp. 170–171).

### Bendahara Seri Maharaja Tun Mutahir

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Bendahara Seri Maharaja, “old Mutahir” in Brown's account of the wedding; Brown also describes him engaging in business (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 159–161).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Head of a Melaka court household with relatives in high office, including the Sri Nara Diraja and Tun Hasan in Brown (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 159, 162–163).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] Brown pairs his household with Tun Tahir's through marriage and casts Mendeliar and the Laksamana as accusers. Pires says merchants, predominantly Gujarati contributors, “took him double the present” in 1509 (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 159–163; Pires II, pp. 254–257).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] Pires makes him a supporter of the attack on Sequeira; Brown has him accept Naina Sura Dewana's gift in a pending dispute and order his retainers not to resist the Sultan's men (Pires II, pp. 255–258; Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 161–163).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] Executed with members of his family by Mahmud's order before Melaka fell (Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 163–167; Pires II, pp. 258–259).
- **Source disagreements:** [Documented fact] Brown calls the treason report false. Pires juxtaposes reports of covert takeover with denials and stresses the king's fear, disfavor after 1509 and confiscation (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 162–164; Pires II, pp. 257–259).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] Neither account fixes his private motive or proves a unified ethnic party under his command. A kinship network is firmer than labeling every associate “Tamil-Muslim” (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 159–163; Pires II, pp. 254–259; see the historiographical reading in [1511-events.md §4.3](1511-events.md#43-why-bendahara-tun-mutahir-was-executed-in-1510)).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Make merchant hearings and access to this household consequential; the purge can abruptly remove a patron without transferring every relationship to his successor (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 161–164).

### Tun Hasan Temenggung

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Temenggung and Mutahir's son in the *Annals* (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 160, 163).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Melaka's Bendahara household (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 163).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] When Mahmud's men come for the family, he is “for fighting” but puts down his weapon on his father's instruction (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 163).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] Brown credits him with repelling a Portuguese attack in the earlier encounter; Pires has a Temenggung oppose killing the newcomers in council despite merchants bringing him a present (Brown, ch. XXI, pp. 157–158; Pires II, pp. 255–256).
- **Fate:** [Scholarly inference] Cortesão's note, following Wilkinson, places Hasan among the purge victims; Brown explicitly names the Bendahara and the Sri Nara Diraja among those killed, but does not name Hasan in the execution sentence (Pires II, p. 256 n. 1; Brown, ch. XXII, p. 163).
- **Source disagreements:** [Documented fact] Pires calls the customs judge the Bendahara's “brother” and calls a councillor “Tumungo Cerina De Raja”; Cortesão suspects a confusion with Tun Tahir. Brown calls Hasan the Bendahara's son (Pires II, pp. 255–256 and n. 1, 273; Brown, ch. XXII, p. 163).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] Do not create an additional named brother-temenggung or give Hasan every line spoken by Pires's unnamed or ambiguously titled officeholder (Pires II, p. 256 n. 1, p. 273; Brown, ch. XXII, p. 163).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Use Hasan as the pre-purge customs connection; put any uncertain speeches into fallible reports, not fixed biography (Pires II, pp. 255–256, 273; Brown, ch. XXII, p. 163).

### Tun Mai Ulat Bulu, Seri Awadana

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Brown calls Tun Mai one of Ahmad's companions and says he was made Temenggung as Sri Awadana (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 166).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Melaka court nobility, in Brown's genealogy descended from Bendahara Paduka Raja (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 166).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] Brown lists him among Ahmad's favorites and says he was also a favorite of Mahmud (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 166).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] Sri Awadana rides with Ahmad and is wounded in the defense of Melaka (Brown, ch. XXIII, pp. 168–169).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] A doctor says his wound is treatable; Brown later mentions Sri Awadana's death in exile and names Tun Narawangsa as the next Temenggong (Brown, chs. XXIII, XXVIII, pp. 169, 192).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] Brown supplies a named Temenggung at the fall; Pires's description of the earlier customs judge is not a competing identification for this episode (Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 166–169; Pires II, p. 273).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] Do not assign a dated transfer of the customs bench on the strength of the narrative sequence alone (Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 163, 166–169).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Use him as a court-to-harbour contact in the defense phase, separate from Hasan (Brown, ch. XXIII, pp. 168–169).

### Tun Tahir, Seri Nara Diraja

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Seri Nara Diraja; Cortesão, citing Wilkinson, identifies him as chief treasury officer (Brown, index, s.v. “Sri Nara 'diraja (III)”; Pires II, p. 256 n. 1).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Bendahara's brother in Brown's execution narrative; his son Tun Ali married Mutahir's daughter (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 159–160, 163).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] He stays with the Bendahara and accepts the royal sentence with him (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 163).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Scholarly inference] Cortesão suggests Tahir may be the councillor whom Pires mislabels “Tumungo Cerina De Raja.” This is not proof that Tahir held that office or gave that advice (Pires II, p. 256 and n. 1).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] Killed in the purge. His wounded son Tun Hamzah survived and later held the same title in exile (Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 163, 170).
- **Source disagreements:** [Documented fact] Brown distinguishes the brother, Sri Nara Diraja, from the son, Tun Hasan Temenggong; Pires's title conflation obscures that distinction (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 163; Pires II, p. 256 and n. 1).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] The texts do not establish Tahir's own merchant clients or dealings at the quay (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 159–163; Pires II, p. 256 n. 1).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Distinguish the treasury patron from the customs judge; later reports can carry Tun Hamzah under the same title but a different character ID (Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 163, 170).

### Bendahara Paduka Tuan (Tun Pikrama; Lubok Batu)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Mutahir's successor, known as Bendahara Lubok Batu; Brown identifies this Paduka Tuan as Tun Pikrama (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 164; p. 245, note 549).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Melaka court nobility, son of Bendahara Paduka Raja (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 164).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] Mahmud insists on appointing him despite his protest; at the fall his family removes him from the fighting against his wishes (Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 164, 169).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] Brown describes an elderly, physically impaired officeholder who still wants to fight for Melaka (Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 164, 169).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] Died after the retreat in Muar and was buried at Lubok Batu (Brown, ch. XXIII, p. 169).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] Pires reports an unnamed Bendahara counseling peace in 1511; it fits the succession in Brown, but is not a named attribution by Pires. Brown's later Paduka Tuan is a different man, Tun Isak Berakah (Pires II, p. 280; Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 164, 169–170; note 793).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] Brown's account does not measure his administrative capacity or show that he failed to act because of age (Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 164, 169).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Reset patronage changes after the purge without turning his impairment into a loyalty or competence penalty (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 164).

### Laksamana Khoja Hasan / Khoja Husain

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Admiral in the purge story. Brown prints “Khoja Husain”; Cortesão's note prints “Khoja Hasan” (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 162–164; Pires II, p. 256 n. 1).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Court officer; Brown links his household to Hang Tuah's family by marriage (Brown, ch. XVIII, p. 142).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] In Brown the admiral and his kinsmen are in favor with Mahmud; he accepts Mendeliar's valuables and relays the accusation (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 162–163).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] Pires's unnamed admiral objects that attacking Sequeira is “contrary to justice”; Brown's named admiral participates in the purge intrigue. Birch's post-fall story has the king waiting on an unnamed admiral sent to Lingga (Pires II, pp. 255–258; Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 162–164; Birch III, ch. XXIX, pp. 128–130).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] Brown reports Mahmud ordered him castrated and subsequently says he died and was buried on Bukit Pantau (Brown, chs. XXII–XXIII, pp. 164, 171).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] Cortesão notes that Pires's portrayal does not sound like Brown's court favorite. The unnamed Lingga admiral cannot securely be matched to Khoja Husain from these passages (Pires II, p. 256 n. 1; Brown, ch. XXII, p. 162; Birch III, ch. XXIX, pp. 128–130).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] His exact status and whereabouts at the siege are unresolved; being offered a present in Pires is not the same as agreeing to the attack (Pires II, pp. 255–258; Brown, ch. XXIII, p. 171; Birch III, ch. XXIX, pp. 128–130).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Give the named admiral a purge role; use an office-level fleet dispatch if a 1511 messenger need not identify its commander (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 162–164; Birch III, ch. XXIX, pp. 128–130).

### Hang Nadim

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] In Brown a royal agent before the fall, later Laksamana after Khoja Husain (Brown, chs. XVIII–XIX, pp. 139–148; ch. XXIII, p. 171).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Melaka court service; married into the families described in Brown's narrative (Brown, ch. XVIII, p. 142; ch. XXIII, p. 171).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] Serves Mahmud on court missions; Brown does not place him among the purge accusers (Brown, chs. XVIII–XIX, XXII, pp. 139–148, 161–164).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Scholarly inference] Brown's earlier missions establish his connection to court, not a dated billet in the siege; Birch uses only the title in the Lingga episode (Brown, chs. XVIII–XIX, XXIII, pp. 139–148, 167–171; Birch III, ch. XXIX, pp. 128–130).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] Appears in continued service after the fall in Brown's narrative (Brown, ch. XXIII, p. 171; ch. XXV, pp. 176–179).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] Brown places his appointment in the exile narrative. Birch supplies no name to justify backdating the appointment to the 1511 fleet (Brown, ch. XXIII, pp. 170–171; Birch III, ch. XXIX, pp. 128–130).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] No secure 1511 command posting in these accounts (Brown, ch. XXIII, pp. 167–171; Birch III, ch. XXIX, pp. 128–130).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] If he appears in the 1509–1511 game, keep optional agent errands fictional and do not style him siege admiral (Brown, chs. XVIII–XIX, XXIII, pp. 139–148, 171).

### Raja Mendeliar (Brown's Raja Mendaliar)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Wealthy “Master of the Port” in Brown, i.e. a shahbandar (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 160; compare Pires II, p. 265).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Brown calls him a settled “man of Kalinga” and has Mutahir call him a Kling; the passage does not establish his religion (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 160–161).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] In dispute with Naina Sura Dewana; creditor to Kittul; seeks the admiral's intervention against Mutahir (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 161–162).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] The *Annals* have him cancel Kittul's debt and bring gold and jewels to the Laksamana with the claim that Mutahir had a throne made for himself (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 162–163).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] Mahmud orders his execution for the false accusation in Brown (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 164).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] His Kalinga origin makes Pires's Bunuaqujlim constituency plausible, but Pires does not name its incumbent or corroborate Brown's accusation narrative (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 160–164; Pires II, pp. 258–259, 265).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] Neither a personal name behind the title nor a precise constituency roster is established here; Keling is not a religion (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 160; Pires II, p. 265).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Use him as the Keling-connected port official, with explicit uncertainty on the full constituency. Let the commercial dispute escalate through personal ties, not community-wide alignment (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 160–164).

### Naina Sura Dewana (Nina Sura Dewana)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Brown calls him the “leading merchant in the city,” not shahbandar (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 161).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Melaka merchant and kinsman of Kittul, whom Brown calls a man of Kalinga; Brown does not explicitly specify Dewana's religion or birthplace (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 161–162).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] His commercial rival in the case is Mendeliar, and he seeks Mutahir's favor (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 161–162).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] He gives Mutahir a bahara of gold while his claim is pending. In Brown it is Kittul who turns that gift into an alleged murder plot (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 161–162).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] The episode does not give Dewana a sentence or subsequent fate (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 161–164).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] Do not merge him with Nina Chatu, who is named in different episodes and sources; neither a similar first element nor a broad trading community identifies the two (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 161–162; Pires II, pp. 258, 281).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] The original terms of the commercial case and his political program are not supplied (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 161–162).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Make the disputed cargo or obligation fictional; preserve the difference between the gift and the allegation (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 161–162).

### Si Kitul (Brown's Kittul)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Debtor and intermediary in Brown; no port office is named (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 162).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] “Man of Kalinga” related to Dewana (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 162).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] A kinsman of one disputant and debtor to the other. Mendeliar tears up his bond after hearing his report (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 162).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] Reports that the gift to the Bendahara was payment to have Mendeliar killed; the *Annals* do not say Dewana gave that instruction (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 161–162).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] Mahmud orders his impalement (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 164).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] The corresponding passage in Pires gives a different explanation for the purge and does not identify this intermediary (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 162–164; Pires II, pp. 257–259).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] His occupation and any continuing informant network are unknown (Brown, ch. XXII, p. 162).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Model the debt as leverage affecting a rumor's route, and let another witness contradict the account (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 161–162).

### Gujarati shahbandar (unnamed)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] “Xabandari of the Guzarates,” the “mainstay of all the merchants of Cambaya” in Birch's version of Araújo's report. Pires calls the Gujarati office the most important of the four (Birch III, p. 70; Pires II, p. 265).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Represents Gujarati/Cambay merchants; his advice contrasts “Moors” and Christians as groups, but these lines provide no personal name or family (Birch III, p. 70).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] Urges the king not to admit the Portuguese as rivals for cargo in the same port and monsoon, and to delay until the fleet has to leave (Birch III, p. 70).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] The advice belongs to 1511. Pires also has an unnamed “captain of the Gujaratees” in the 1509 plot and a Gujarat captain advocating war in 1511 (Birch III, p. 70; Pires II, pp. 257, 279).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] Neither account follows a named incumbent to an individual fate (Birch III, pp. 70, 97–99; Pires II, pp. 279–282).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] A shahbandar and a captain can both be politically influential without being the same person. These accounts do not establish whether the captain passages refer to one individual or whether either was the shahbandar (Birch III, p. 70; Pires II, pp. 257, 279).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] No securely named Gujarati merchant or captain in the checked Melaka episodes, not a claim about all surviving records (Pires II, pp. 254–257, 269–270, 279–280; Birch III, pp. 69–70, 97–99).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Keep the shahbandar's attested advice; make any personal name, family details and captain relationship explicitly fictional (Birch III, p. 70; Pires II, pp. 257, 265, 279).

### Nina Chatu (Ninachatu / Nina Chetu)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Merchant and resident; later governor of the Klings and Bendahara in Portuguese Melaka in Pires. Neither witness calls him the pre-conquest Keling shahbandar (Pires II, pp. 258 n. 1, 265, 281; Birch III, ch. X, pp. 44–46, ch. XXIX, pp. 128–129).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Birch calls him “a Hindoo resident of Malaca”; the Barros passage quoted by Cortesão calls him “a native of Kling” (Birch III, pp. 44–46; Pires II, p. 258 n. 1).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] He helps Araújo and other prisoners; after the conquest Araújo presents him to Albuquerque and asks that he be honored (Pires II, p. 258; Birch III, pp. 125–127).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] Pires describes payments on behalf of captives; Birch says he enabled their letter to be sent, then advised on coinage and participated in a trading venture alongside Cogequirmani after the conquest (Pires II, pp. 258, 281; Birch III, pp. 44–46, 128–129, 137–140, 162–163).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] Pires says he died as the king of Kampar was coming to rule; he records rival reports of grief and poison (Pires II, pp. 287–288).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] Cortesão favors poison and questions Pires's January 1514 date against the later chronicle chronology, proposing January 1515 as a possible correction; other chroniclers tell a self-immolation story. Do not present a uniform date and manner of death (Pires II, pp. 287–288 and editorial note).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] Personal aid and subsequent patronage are better attested than a master plan to displace every Gujarati merchant (Pires II, pp. 258, 281, 287–288; Birch III, pp. 125–129).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] A recurring broker whose requests, referrals and debts open different routes before and after conquest; dialogue and individual cargo covers are invented (Pires II, pp. 258, 281; Birch III, pp. 125–129).

### Utimutiraja (Utemutaraja / Utamutarraja)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Wealthy Upeh merchant and headman with a large household that included enslaved people; appointed chief over the Muslims in Birch's immediate post-fall account (Birch III, pp. 109–110, 128–129, 151–152).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Birch says he was born a “Javanese Hindoo,” moved to Melaka, and had long since become Muslim (“Moor” in the translation) (Birch III, pp. 151–152).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] He sought a Portuguese safeguard, admitted supplying the king some labor for stockades, and later supplied the Portuguese with men against the prince at Muar. Birch reports complaints about his control of supplies and an alleged agreement with “Alaoadim” (Birch III, pp. 109–110, 129–131, 143–148).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] Pires names his son in the 1509 plotting and father and son among opponents of peace in 1511; Birch records the father's separate negotiations and the son's support for the king during the fighting (Pires II, pp. 255, 257, 280; Birch III, pp. 109–110, 125–127).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] Beheaded by the Portuguese with three relatives after the conquest. Birch calls them a son, son-in-law and grandson; his widow unsuccessfully sought clemency and supported resistance afterward (Pires II, p. 281; Birch III, pp. 147–152).
- **Source disagreements:** [Documented fact] Pires attributes the executions to “Malayan intrigues” and cloves; Birch's longer case includes letters, supplies and seizures of property and people. The charges are claims in a conqueror's account, not independent findings (Pires II, p. 281; Birch III, pp. 143–150).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] Birch's age and household counts are estimates in a hostile portrayal. The safeguard is not evidence of sustained neutrality, and his motives cannot be reduced to a single betrayal (Birch III, pp. 109–110, 125–129, 151–152).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Track his household's safety, the court's demands and supply access separately; the same agreement need not bind his son (Birch III, pp. 109–110, 125–129, 143–145).

### Utimutiraja's widow (unnamed)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Wife and later widow of the Upeh headman; no independent office or personal name is supplied (Birch III, pp. 147, 149–152).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Utimutiraja's Upeh household; Birch does not establish her own birthplace or religion (Birch III, pp. 109–110, 149–152).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] She enlists Kadir first on behalf of her family and then against the Klings and Chinese she blamed (Birch III, pp. 149–152).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] Seeks clemency, buries the dead, and commits gold and household resources to retaliation in the post-execution sequence (Birch III, pp. 149–152).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] Birch last has her leave the city with Kadir; no later fate given there (Birch III, pp. 151–152).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] Pires ties Kadir's revolt to Utimutiraja's money, but does not specify the widow's role; Birch supplies her agency as well as the attribution of blame (Pires II, p. 282; Birch III, pp. 149–152).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] No basis in these passages for identifying her with the unnamed Javanese woman in Birch's account of Sequeira's escape (Birch III, pp. 150–151).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Keep the household's interests active after Utimutiraja's death; leave the widow unnamed or mark any supplied name as fiction (Birch III, pp. 149–152).

### Pate Acoo

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Pires names him as Utimutiraja's son and a participant in political counsel, not as an appointed shahbandar (Pires II, p. 280).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Son of the Javanese headman of Upeh (Pires II, pp. 255, 280; Birch III, pp. 109–110).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] Listed alongside his father, the Gujaratis and court advisers resisting peace in 1511 (Pires II, p. 280).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Scholarly inference] He is a plausible identification for the unnamed son whom Pires involves in 1509 and for the son wounded fighting for the king in Birch's 1511 account, but neither episode supplies the name (Pires II, pp. 255, 257, 280; Birch III, pp. 125–127).
- **Fate:** [Scholarly inference] A son was executed; the execution passages do not supply his name (Pires II, p. 281; Birch III, pp. 147–151).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] Pires's naming anchors one appearance; Birch cannot independently establish that every mention of a son refers to Acoo (Pires II, pp. 280–281; Birch III, pp. 109, 125–127, 147–151).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] No independent office, personal cargo or dialogue is securely attributable (Pires II, p. 280).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Give Acoo an individual relationship with the court rather than using him as a proxy for his father's promises (Pires II, p. 280; Birch III, pp. 125–127).

### Tuan Colascar (Pires's Tuam Colaxcar; Birch's Tuão Calascar)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Post-conquest leader of Hilir (“Her” in Pires; “the other part of the city” in Birch) (Pires II, pp. 259 n. 2, 281–282; Birch III, pp. 166–167).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] “A Javanese by birth” in Birch (Birch III, p. 166).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] Receives authority under the Portuguese captain's superior jurisdiction, separately from the Upeh leadership (Pires II, pp. 281–282; Birch III, pp. 166–167).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] Attested in the administrative settlement after conquest. The cited passages do not give him a 1509 action or a role in the siege (Pires II, pp. 281–282; Birch III, pp. 166–167).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] The appointment is the last notice of him in these passages; no death or removal is given (Pires II, pp. 281–282; Birch III, pp. 166–167).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] The two accounts broadly agree on his quarter; they do not establish that he held it under Mahmud, or that its interests always opposed Upeh's (Pires II, pp. 281–282; Birch III, pp. 166–167).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] Pre-fall rank, trade specialization and any personal rivalry with Utimutiraja are unknown (Pires II, pp. 281–282; Birch III, pp. 166–167).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Use a separate Hilir contact. If Colascar fills that role before the conquest, label the earlier role invented (Pires II, pp. 281–282).

### Patih Kadir (Pate Quedir / Patequitir)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Pires names him as governor of Upeh after Utimutiraja; Birch introduces him as the widow's intermediary (Pires II, pp. 281–282; Birch III, pp. 149–151).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Javanese (Pires II, p. 282; Birch III, pp. 149–150).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] Birch has the widow enlist him and fund reprisals against Klings and Chinese whom she blamed. Pires likewise connects his revolt to Utimutiraja's money (Pires II, p. 282; Birch III, pp. 150–151).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] In the immediate post-execution sequence he petitions for clemency, then leads armed resistance; no 1509 role is specified (Birch III, pp. 149–152; Pires II, p. 282).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] Pires says he was expelled and returned to Java; Birch says he obtained safe conduct but chose not to return to Melaka (Pires II, p. 282; Birch III, pp. 151–152).
- **Source disagreements:** [Documented fact] Birch's later list assigns Upeh to “Regunecerage,” not Kadir; Pires assigns it to Kadir in his account. Do not assume an uninterrupted succession or identify Kadir with Pati Unus, whom Pires discusses separately (Birch III, pp. 166–167; Pires II, p. 282).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] Exact tenure and the distribution of support inside Upeh remain uncertain (Pires II, p. 282; Birch III, pp. 149–152, 166–167).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Introduce him through Utimutiraja's household, then expose a separate chain of retaliation and shipping disruption in an aftermath module (Pires II, p. 282; Birch III, pp. 149–152).

### Regimo Diraja (Pires's Regimo de Raja)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Pires calls him Tumunguo and gives him the governorship of “Lugoes, Parsees and Malays” under the Portuguese (Pires II, p. 281).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Described by Pires as “a Lugao Moor,” i.e. Muslim and of the Luzon community (Pires II, pp. 265, 281).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] Entrusted with the named communities after conquest; no personal rival is stated there (Pires II, p. 281).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] Appears in Pires's account of the post-conquest settlement; no pre-conquest action or cargo is specified (Pires II, pp. 280–282).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] No further fate recorded in Pires's appointment passage (Pires II, pp. 281–282).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] Birch's roster has a Muslim governor identified only as a Caciz and an Upeh governor “Regunecerage.” Neither should be silently equated with Pires's named Temenggung (Birch III, pp. 166–167; Pires II, p. 281).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] His trade specialty and pre-conquest post are unverified; do not back-project these Portuguese-era jurisdictions onto the sultanate (Pires II, pp. 265, 281).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Use him as a post-fall authority linking several communities, not as the sultanate's Javanese/Luzon shahbandar (Pires II, pp. 265, 281).

### Raja Abdullah of Kampar (Pires's Audela / Audelaa)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] King of Kampar, Mahmud's nephew and son-in-law in Pires; later governor under the title rendered Maneco Bumi (Pires II, pp. 254 n. 2, 281, 288–289).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Malay ruler of neighboring Kampar, not a resident merchant leader in the conquest accounts (Pires II, pp. 281, 289; Brown, ch. XXIV, pp. 171–172).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] Brown reports he refused Mahmud's overlordship and sought Portuguese protection (Brown, ch. XXIV, pp. 171–172).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] Pires places the kings of Kampar and Pahang with the flight from Melaka and says Abdullah fled to Kampar after Mahmud threatened him (Pires II, pp. 280–281).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] He took over after Nina Chatu under the Portuguese. Cortesão cites Portuguese chroniclers for his beheading (Pires II, pp. 287–289 and notes).
- **Source disagreements:** [Documented fact] Brown says he was sent to Goa and then Portugal; Cortesão's Portuguese accounts give execution in Melaka. These are alternative accounts of his removal, not stages supported by either story (Brown, ch. XXIV, pp. 171–173; Pires II, p. 289 editorial note).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] No secure basis here for making him a 1511 port patron or assigning one motive for his break with Mahmud (Pires II, pp. 280–281; Brown, ch. XXIV, pp. 171–172).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Keep him a regional relationship, then use competing reports for the consequences of his later appointment (Pires II, pp. 287–289; Brown, ch. XXIV, pp. 171–172).

### Cojeabrahem (Birch: “Master Abraham”)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Applicant for the office of Quitoal; Birch does not say in this scene that he received it (Birch III, pp. 147–148).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Described as Muslim and Persian (Birch III, p. 147).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] Called a “great friend of Utemutaraja”; approached by Albuquerque under the pretext of consulting the inhabitants about appointments (Birch III, pp. 147–148).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] Brings the principals to the fortress, where the Portuguese disarm them and Araújo reads the charges (Birch III, pp. 147–148).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] This episode records no appointment, punishment or later fate for him (Birch III, pp. 147–152).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] The arrest scene is a Birch account, not corroborated by Pires's short notice; it does not prove that Cojeabrahem intended to betray his friend (Birch III, pp. 147–148; Pires II, p. 281).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] No cargo, merchant office or Persian community leadership is established; friendship and petitioning are the attested connections (Birch III, pp. 147–148).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Use him as an office-seeking intermediary; any private foreknowledge or merchant business should be labeled fiction (Birch III, pp. 147–148).

### Cogequirmani (Birch's gloss: Khoja Kirmani)

- **Role/title:** [Documented fact] Named participant in the eastern trading venture and captain of its commercial junk (Birch III, pp. 162–163).
- **Community:** [Documented fact] Birch's text calls him “a Hindoo” with family settled in Melaka; the editor glosses the name as “the Merchant of Kirman” (Birch III, pp. 162–163 and note).
- **Allegiances & rivals:** [Documented fact] Receives a share of merchandise alongside Nina Chatu in the Portuguese-organized venture; no rival is named (Birch III, pp. 162–163).
- **1509–1511 actions:** [Documented fact] In the post-conquest dispatch he sails ahead of the main fleet (Birch III, pp. 162–163).
- **Fate:** [Documented fact] The dispatch account does not give a personal endpoint after sailing (Birch III, pp. 162–164).
- **Source disagreements:** [Scholarly inference] Do not turn an editorial gloss into proof of Persian ancestry or use it to override the narrative's community description. Nor does his title identify him with Cojeabrahem (Birch III, pp. 147–148, 162–163).
- **Invented/unknown:** [Scholarly inference] An earlier Melaka career and a precise origin are not established in this passage (Birch III, pp. 162–163).
- **Suggested game use:** [Gameplay abstraction] Use as a late departure contact; create any pre-conquest business as fiction (Birch III, pp. 162–163).

## 4. What the game would invent

| Character slot | Proposed treatment |
|---|---|
| Gujarati shahbandar / ship captain | [Gameplay abstraction] “Cambay Shahbandar” preserves the attested office and advice without supplying a historical-sounding name; an optional named Cambay captain and any friendship between them are fiction (Birch III, p. 70; Pires II, pp. 257, 265, 279). |
| Eastern shahbandars | [Gameplay abstraction] “Upeh Reception Broker” and “Eastern Junks Shahbandar” are fictional persistent officeholders inspired by Pires's unnamed Javanese/Luzon-facing and Chinese-facing posts. Neither should inherit Kadir's, Colascar's or Regimo's documented post-conquest identity (Pires II, pp. 265, 281–282). |
| Keling-connected shahbandar | [Gameplay abstraction] Use Mendeliar with the constituency caveat above, or a fictional “Coromandel Reception Broker” if the game needs a continuing post after his death; do not turn Nina Chatu or Dewana into that incumbent (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 160–164; Pires II, pp. 265, 281). |
| Chinese and Pegu traders | [Gameplay abstraction] Use archetypes such as “Junk Captain with the Pepper Consignment” and “Pegu Provision Broker.” Any names, cargo brands, family ties and return visits for those recurring characters are fictional (Birch III, pp. 113–115, 125–126, 130, 141–142). |
| Other western and Bay of Bengal merchants | [Gameplay abstraction] A “Bengal Cloth Factor” or “Ormuz Correspondent” can support recurring trades. Do not present Cojeabrahem as a documented Persian merchant representative merely to fill a roster slot (Pires II, pp. 265, 268–269; Birch III, pp. 147–148). |
| Household agents and mortal endpoints | [Gameplay abstraction] Use fictional couriers, clerks and protégés for repeatable jobs, open-ended careers or alternate fates. Keep documented endpoints in the historical mode, disputed endpoints in attributed reports, and silence as silence; never invent an ethnicity-based loyalty modifier. Portraits, relationship meters, exact speech and player-facing deal terms are design choices (Brown, ch. XXII, pp. 162–164; Pires II, pp. 281–282, 287–289; Birch III, pp. 147–152). |
